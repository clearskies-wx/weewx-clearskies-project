## Setup

- **Boundary station used:** `46222` (San Pedro Channel), confirmed from the running service's
  journal (`weewx-clearskies-marine`, 2026-07-30 19:36 UTC): `select_boundary_stations` resolved
  W side = `[46256 (3.4km), 46222 (7.7km), 46253 (5.2km)]`, S side = `[46223 (16.9km)]`, product
  `ocean`, and the actual fetched cycle was **`gfswave` 2026-07-30 **12Z** (the 18Z cycle 403'd —
  not yet published — the service fell back to 12Z, matching what this script also fetched).
  46222 was chosen over the S-side 46223 because it is **both** a boundary station our pipeline
  fetches **and** a real NDBC directional buoy (San Pedro Channel) at the identical lat/lon
  (33.62N/-118.32W per the `.spec` header) — this removes a spatial confound between "our
  boundary source" and "the real buoy." 46223 was independently spot-checked too (see below) and
  shows the same shape, so the finding is not station-specific.
- **Valid time (shared across all sources):** **2026-07-30T19:00:00Z** — the WW3 forecast hour
  (f007 from the 12Z cycle) landed exactly on NDBC's most recent published spectral record for
  46222 (`2026 07 30 19 00`), so no interpolation/offset was needed between the model forecast and
  the buoy observation.
- **Surfline baseline time:** the operator's TA-C20 screenshot is timestamped only "2026-07-30" in
  the concerns log, not to the hour. It may not be the same hour as 19:00Z — flagged, not resolved.
- **Independent partitioning method (not `decompose_spectrum`/`watershed_partitions`):** a plain
  valley-following walk on each source's 1-D frequency marginal (rank local maxima by height,
  expand each into a contiguous band while the marginal keeps decreasing, stop at a valley or an
  already-claimed bin). Direction per band = energy-weighted circular mean of the band's own
  direction data (2-D matrix argmax-weighted mean for WW3/our-boundary; NDBC's `alpha1` weighted by
  `.data_spec` energy for the buoy, since NDBC's realtime spectral files only publish a 1-D
  energy(freq) + per-frequency mean direction, not a full 2-D matrix — this is exactly the
  fallback the task spec allows ("1-D energy(freq) + dominant direction per band is enough").
  Two threshold settings were run to check sensitivity: `min_frac=0.08` (peaks below 8% of the
  strongest peak's amplitude discarded) and `min_frac=0.02` (more permissive). Findings below use
  `min_frac=0.02` because at `0.08` the windsea band (real, ~30% of buoy's total Hs) was dropped
  purely by a peak-HEIGHT threshold, not because the underlying valley wasn't there — the `0.02`
  run is the one that matches physical reality without discarding a real, energy-significant band.

## Verdict

**(a) is definitively RULED OUT.** Our conversion is a byte-for-byte-faithful passthrough at this
station/time: raw WW3 Hs = 0.894 m, our-converted-boundary Hs = 0.894 m (**0.00% difference**), same
2 bands, same periods (14.5 s / 5.6 s), same directions (179° / 267°), same per-band Hs (0.826 m /
0.343 m). `ww3_spectrum_to_swan_boundary()` does not lose, merge, or distort any structure that is
present in the raw `.spec` file. If a train is "missing" downstream of the boundary writer, it was
never in the raw WW3 data to begin with.

**Primary finding — train/period fidelity (what T3.0 targets): (c), a real WW3-model limit, not a
station/product/cycle selection error (b).** The raw WW3 frequency marginal at 46222 (and
independently re-checked at 46223, the plan's other candidate boundary station — see below) is a
**single, smooth, unimodal hump from ~9.7 s to ~28 s peaking at 14.5 s, with no valley anywhere in
that range** (printed marginal in the script's `--debug` output). The real NDBC buoy, sampling the
identical patch of ocean at the identical hour, resolves **three separate peaks within that same
range** (11.8 s, 13.3 s, 15.4 s, all within a similar ~161–171° direction band). WW3's own model
spectrum genuinely does not carry that sub-structure as separate peaks at this cycle/station/time —
it is smoothed into one broad swell hump. This is not a wrong station or cycle: 46222 (7.7 km) is a
qualifying boundary station our own pipeline actually selected and fetched this cycle; the S-side
station 46223 (16.9 km, the one the plan's prior trace referenced) shows the **identical shape**
(single unimodal hump 9.7–28 s peaking at 14.5 s, E-values scaled up but no valley) — ruling out
"wrong station" as the explanation. The cycle (12Z, `gfswave`) is the same cycle our own pipeline
fell back to (18Z 403'd/unpublished) — not a stale-cycle selection bug, the correct current cycle.

**Aggregate energy — swell-vs-windsea split roughly holds up across all three real sources.** When
the buoy's fine sub-peaks are combined by RSS into "swell family" and "windsea family," WW3's own
2-band split (0.826 m swell / 0.343 m windsea) tracks the buoy's aggregate (≈0.83 m / ≈0.39 m)
within a few percent — the windsea number in particular is close to Surfline's own windsea entry
(0.37 m @ 6 s W 273° vs WW3's 0.343 m @ 5.6 s W 267°, a ~7% Hs difference and single-digit degree
direction difference). **Where WW3/buoy diverge sharply from Surfline is the SWELL total**: WW3 and
the buoy both put total swell energy around 0.83 m, while Surfline's two swell entries combine (RSS)
to ≈1.28 m — a genuine, large residual gap on the swell side that neither the raw WW3 fetch nor our
conversion can explain, since both real, independent, model-agnostic sources (NOAA's own model AND
a physical buoy) agree with each other and disagree with Surfline by roughly the same margin. This
could be: (i) the validation-time mismatch noted above (Surfline's screenshot timestamp is unknown
to the hour — a stronger swell pulse at a different hour of the 3-day window is possible and not
ruled out here); (ii) Surfline's LOTUS blending a different/nearer buoy or a proprietary correction;
or (iii) a genuine model under-prediction on the swell side at this specific cycle. This report does
not adjudicate between those — it only establishes that the gap is **not** introduced by our fetch
or our boundary-conversion code.

## Explicit separation: train/period fidelity vs magnitude (per task instruction)

This measurement is about **train/period fidelity at the boundary**, not the ~2× magnitude gap
TA-C20 documents. **Boundary faithfulness does not by itself explain TA-C20's magnitude gap:**
TA-C20's own root-cause stack (handoff placed shoreward of the SWAN break point; headline metric is
max-single-partition, not the combined broken wave; a residual shoaling/refraction or scale-
convention gap) is entirely downstream of the boundary and operates on SWAN's OWN computed field,
not on the raw WW3 input this report measures. Even if the WW3 boundary carried Surfline's full
1.28 m swell total intact all the way to the 1-D handoff, TA-C20's causes #1 and #2 would still
apply and would still roughly halve the served number. Conversely, closing TA-C20's causes will not
manufacture a third swell train the raw WW3 input does not contain.
