# Worldwide recreational species audit

## Scope

The matrix covers the Great Lakes and world oceans/seas. It excludes inland
lakes, rivers, and streams outside the Great Lakes. A species is retained only
when sources support ordinary recreational shore, pier, kayak, small-boat,
handline, rod-and-reel, jig, personal-pot, trap, or equivalent access. It does
not include commercial-only, industrial, or heavily specialised targets.

## Research coverage

Completed regional passes include the Great Lakes; North Atlantic and Europe;
Mediterranean, Black Sea, North Sea, and Baltic; Africa; the Indian Ocean;
Arabian Sea and Gulf; Asia; Pacific Islands; Australia and New Zealand;
Caribbean; Central America; Eastern Pacific; and South America. The audit
covers finfish, cartilaginous fish, flatfish, cephalopods, and mobile
crustaceans.

## Conservation handling

Exact taxa classified Vulnerable, Endangered, or Critically Endangered are
excluded from the selectable matrix. Examples found during this pass include
great white shark, Atlantic halibut does not qualify under the standard-tackle
method rule, all researched sturgeon targets, great white shark, Cubera snapper,
Nassau grouper, European eel, lake sturgeon, and American eel. Near Threatened
species remain only with the operational `near_threatened` flag.

## Source classes

The audit uses FishBase and SeaLifeBase exact species records for taxonomy,
range, depth, temperature and IUCN results; national fisheries agencies and
state/provincial fisheries authorities for ordinary recreational method; and
FAO or peer-reviewed sources where they provide the needed regional or
biological evidence. Operational tide, pressure, time, and solunar entries use
the approved bounded profile conventions only where an exact source does not
provide a species-specific measure.
